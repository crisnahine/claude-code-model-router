# Review protocol: Brightline and reading comprehension

**Question.** Does the Brightline reading program improve the reading comprehension of grade 3 pupils, compared with usual classroom teaching?

## Which studies count

- Design: a randomised trial, or a quasi-experimental study with a matched comparison group. Both are eligible.
- Population: pupils in grade 3 (ages 8 to 9). A study of a wider age range is eligible only if it reports grade 3 results on their own, and then only those results are used.
- Intervention: Brightline as published, delivered in school for at least 10 weeks.
- Outcome: a standardised test of reading comprehension. Other reading skills, such as decoding, fluency or vocabulary, are not this review's outcome.
- A study whose paper has been retracted is excluded, whatever it found. Check the journal notices; a correction that leaves the findings standing is not a retraction.

Exclude a study only for one of these reasons, named exactly:

- `retracted`
- `different_outcome`
- `different_population`
- `no_comparison_group`

A study is not excluded because its result disagrees with the others, because it is small, or because its effect is not significant. Every eligible study goes into the pooled estimate.

## Which studies support the claim

An included study supports the claim when its comprehension effect is positive and its 95% confidence interval lies wholly above zero.

## Pooling

Pool the included studies' effect sizes (Hedges' g on comprehension) as a mean weighted by each study's analysed sample size. Report the direction of the pooled evidence as:

- `benefit` when the pooled effect is above +0.05,
- `harm` when it is below -0.05,
- `no_clear_effect` otherwise.
